San Pedro de Atacama: the practical guide

The driest desert on Earth packs geysers, turquoise lagoons, Mars-like valleys and the clearest night sky on the planet around a small adobe town at 2,400 m.

The must-dos

1. Tatio geysers

The highest geothermal field in the world (4,320 m), reached at 6 a.m. and below freezing: the fumaroles only show up in the dawn cold. It is the earliest excursion and the one that demands the most acclimatization — never do it on your first day.

2. Valle de la Luna

Fifteen minutes from town: dunes, salt ridges and stone amphitheatres that turn red at sunset. It is the perfect excursion for your arrival day because it sits almost at the town's altitude.

3. Altiplanic lagoons and Piedras Rojas

Miscanti and Miñiques (4,100 m) mirroring the volcanoes, the Atacama salt flat with flamingos at the Chaxa lagoon, and Piedras Rojas as the closing postcard. A full day, and the most scenic of them all.

4. Stargazing tour

Atacama has the cleanest sky on the planet — which is why the world's largest observatories are here. The night tours with telescopes and a guide are worth every dollar; avoid nights with a full moon.

5. Cejar or Baltinache lagoons

Float in water saltier than the Dead Sea (Cejar) or walk the seven hidden lagoons (Baltinache, quieter and more remote). Either one is a half-day trip.

Essential logistics

  • Getting there: fly to Calama (CJC) + a 1 h 20 min transfer to town.
  • Altitude: order your excursions from lowest to highest: Luna → Cejar → lagoons → Tatio.
  • Weather: 25 °C by day, below freezing at dawn at Tatio: layers and a hat even in summer.
  • Cash: community entrance fees are paid in cash; the town's ATMs run dry in high season.
  • Altiplanic winter: in January-February it can rain on the altiplano and briefly close roads; keep a plan B in your itinerary.

FAQ

How many days?

Four is ideal, three is tight: Valle de la Luna on arrival day, Tatio on the second or third, altiplanic lagoons as a full day, stargazing on any moonless night.

Is the altitude a problem?

The town, no; Tatio and Piedras Rojas do require acclimatizing first. Stay hydrated and take excursions in ascending order of altitude.

Book before arriving?

In high season yes (Tatio and stargazing sell out); in low season a day's notice in town is enough.
